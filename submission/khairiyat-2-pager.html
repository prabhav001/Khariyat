Khairiyat (ख़ैरियत): no unresolved household without an accepted next step

1. What problem will Khairiyat solve?

The moment. The morning after half of all people vanished, the secretary of a (fictional) 160-flat Gurugram society must answer two questions for every flat: has anyone reached this household, and does anyone here need help? The loss is uneven: one home has lost a carer, another a parent, another no one. Silent flats prove nothing: a flat may be empty, or a child or bedridden adult may be unable to answer. Guards and volunteers are fewer too, and reports arrive scattered and contradictory.

The failure I target is follow-up that silently disappears: a flat marked OK on a child’s word, a volunteer who never said yes, a missed deadline, a service called but never committed, a rumour treated as fact. In 2018 societies coordinated through WhatsApp groups, which carry messages but no per-flat status [1] and spread rumours widely enough that WhatsApp limited forwarding in India in July 2018 [2], and through spreadsheets, which hold status but do not enforce follow-through.

Why this problem. I have lived in apartment societies like this since birth, so the guard room, intercom, RWA and residents’ WhatsApp group that Khairiyat builds on are the setting I understand best. I also scored six Delhi-NCR problems (Table 1). Hospital transfers scored highest but depend on bed capacity and government ownership; society roll-call scored lower on severity and evidence, but highest where software itself closes the gap and an RWA can adopt it on day one. Its main risk, a well-run spreadsheet being enough, is what my validation tests.

2. Who am I building it for?

Primary user: the roll-call coordinator of one large Delhi-NCR housing society, an RWA office-bearer or volunteer working from the society office on one laptop or phone: not technical, under pressure, possibly bereaved. Affected but not using it: wardens, guards and volunteers who receive task messages; residents and relatives who send information and receive totals-only updates. Initial scope: an organised high-rise society with a flat register, guard room and intercom (assumptions), which favours better-off societies.

3. What is Khairiyat, and how does it solve the problem?

I propose a single-device coordination desk built on one rule: every unresolved item has a next step that a named person has accepted, with a deadline, and nothing closes without evidence (Figure 1).

  • Two separate records per unit (lower lane). Confirmed never means everyone is safe.
  • Accepted ownership. Unaccepted work is not covered; a NO returns units to the queue; overdue work keeps its owner until extended with a reason or reassigned.
  • No invented evidence. Strict bulk replies: a missing channel, free text or a typo is flagged, never guessed. Corrections are added, never overwritten.
  • Safe outputs. Volunteer messages forbid forcing entry and say to call emergency services directly; Khairiyat never calls or posts anything. Society updates are totals only.

Why not WhatsApp plus a well-run Google Sheet? WhatsApp stays the messaging channel. A Sheet can hold the same columns but does not block a confirmation without a basis, reopen a gap when a volunteer declines or a deadline passes, or keep history instead of overwriting cells. It is better for simultaneous editors and familiarity; whether Khairiyat’s guardrails outweigh that is what I must test.

The prototype (one HTML file, fictional data) has an 18-step guided walkthrough that ends honestly: 129 of 160 households confirmed, the other 31 with accepted owners and deadlines, and two welfare cases still open with their reasons.

4. How will I know it is working?

Primary metric: next-step coverage. It shows whether follow-up is under control, not that anyone is safe, so it always appears beside the unconfirmed and open-case counts (Table 2).

Validation I propose (nothing has been run). (1) Think-aloud sessions with about five RWA volunteers. (2) Coordinator pairs run the same scripted scenario, with ten identical test cases, in Khairiyat and in a well-run Google Sheet. I continue only if Khairiyat shows fewer incorrect closures or fewer unowned or overdue tasks without being materially slower; otherwise I publish the Sheet template and printable floor sheets instead. (3) A drill in one consenting tower.

5. Risks and limits

  • It tracks units, not people, and is not a missing-person system, emergency service or triage tool. Full coverage does not mean the emergency is over, and assigning everything to the desk can game it.
  • One device, no accounts or encryption. Health details are sensitive personal data under India’s 2011 IT rules [3], so a real version needs authentication, encryption and retention rules.

A2. Facts, assumptions and hypotheses

Facts with sources: the three items above.

Fictional premise and scenario assumptions: half of all people vanished at once and unevenly; the society, its 160 flats, about 550 former residents, every person and every message are invented; the society has a flat register, a guard room with intercom, a laptop or phone at the desk, and enough willing volunteers; mobile networks are congested but checks inside the society do not depend on them.

Untested product hypotheses: a guided workflow produces fewer incorrect closures and fewer unowned or overdue follow-ups than a well-run spreadsheet, without being materially slower; coordinators can learn it under pressure; volunteers will reply YES or NO to a structured message.

Problem-selection scores (out of 100, my own weighted judgement): hospital transfers 83; society roll-call 77; infant feeding 75; construction workers 69; water tankers 65; older adults alone 64. Criteria and weights: urgency (10), severity (15), user specificity (10), evidence (15), software makes the difference (15), day-one feasibility (10), adoption (10), differentiation (10), prototype shows the workflow (5).

No research is claimed. No interviews, surveys, usability sessions or pilots have been conducted. The problem-selection scores are my own judgement against stated criteria, not measurements.
